Why Convert kWh Back to M3?

There are several practical reasons why you might need to reverse the gas conversion and find out how many cubic metres correspond to a given kWh value:

  • Checking your gas bill: Energy suppliers charge in kWh but your meter reads in m³. Working backwards lets you verify the m³ figures on your bill are correct.
  • Energy auditing: If you know your energy consumption target in kWh, this converter tells you how many cubic metres of gas you're allowed to use.
  • Budgeting: Convert a monthly kWh budget into cubic metres to monitor your meter reading directly.
  • Comparing tariffs: When comparing suppliers quoting in different units, this conversion creates a like-for-like comparison.
Reverse Ofgem Formula
m³ = (kWh × 3.6) ÷ (Calorific Value × 1.02264)

How the Reverse Conversion Works

The kWh to m³ conversion is simply the inverse of the Ofgem forward formula. Because: kWh = m³ × CV × 1.02264 ÷ 3.6, we can rearrange to get: m³ = kWh × 3.6 ÷ (CV × 1.02264).

This reverse formula is exact — there is no approximation. If you convert m³ to kWh and then back again, you will get the same m³ value (subject to rounding). The key variable is still the calorific value: use 39.5 kWh/m³ for estimates or your bill's exact CV for precision.

Example 1

Convert 224 kWh to m³, CV 39.5

(224 × 3.6) ÷ (39.5 × 1.02264)
= 19.96 m³

Example 2

Convert 1000 kWh to m³, CV 39.5

(1000 × 3.6) ÷ (39.5 × 1.02264)
= 89.44 m³

Example 3

Convert 5000 kWh to m³, CV 40.2

(5000 × 3.6) ÷ (40.2 × 1.02264)
= 437.27 m³

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the formula to convert kWh to m3?
The reverse Ofgem formula is: m³ = (kWh × 3.6) ÷ (Calorific Value × 1.02264). Using the UK average CV of 39.5: m³ = (kWh × 3.6) ÷ (39.5 × 1.02264) = kWh × 0.08944.
How many m3 is 1000 kWh of gas?
Using CV 39.5: (1000 × 3.6) ÷ (39.5 × 1.02264) = 89.44 m³. This varies slightly by calorific value — if your bill shows CV 40.0, then 1000 kWh = 88.33 m³.
Can I use this to verify my gas bill?
Yes. Find the kWh figure on your bill, enter it here with your bill's CV, and compare the result to your actual meter reading difference. If the numbers match (within 1–2%), your bill calculation is correct.
Why do I need to know the calorific value?
The calorific value (CV) measures how much energy is in each m³ of gas. Without it, you can't accurately convert between volume and energy. Your bill's CV is the most accurate one to use — the 39.5 default is a good estimate if you don't have a bill to hand.
Is this calculation the same for all UK gas suppliers?
The formula is the same (Ofgem-mandated), but calorific values vary by region. British Gas, Octopus, EDF and all UK licensed suppliers must use the actual measured CV for their area, which is why your bill shows a specific CV rather than the average 39.5.
