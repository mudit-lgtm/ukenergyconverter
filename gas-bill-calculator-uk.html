How UK Gas Billing Works

UK gas bills are calculated using four main components: the amount of gas used (in kWh), your unit rate, the standing charge, and VAT. The kWh figure is derived from your meter reading using the Ofgem conversion formula.

Your unit rate (in pence per kWh) is set by your energy tariff. Under Ofgem's price cap system, the maximum unit rate changes quarterly. The standing charge is a fixed daily fee that covers the cost of maintaining the gas network, regardless of how much gas you use.

VAT on domestic gas is charged at the reduced rate of 5% (not the standard 20% that applies to most goods and services). This reduced rate is a longstanding policy for domestic energy to keep household bills lower.

Ofgem Price Cap

The Ofgem price cap is a quarterly limit on the maximum unit rate and standing charge that energy suppliers can charge. It does not cap the total bill — it caps the rate. So if you use more gas, your bill will still be higher even under the cap.

The price cap is set based on wholesale energy costs and changes in January, April, July, and October each year. Always check the current cap on Ofgem's website and compare to the rates on your bill.
