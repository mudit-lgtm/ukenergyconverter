🔍 How to Identify Your Meter Type

📏

Metric Meter (m³)

Displays reading followed by m³ or shows the letter M. Numbers typically read 0000–9999. Most meters installed after 1994.

📐

Imperial Meter (ft³)

Shows reading in ft³ or CF. Often has 4 dials with numbers reading higher (e.g. 50000). Common in older UK properties.

Conversion Formulas Explained

📏 Metric (m³) Formula

kWh = m³ × CV × 1.02264 ÷ 3.6

Direct application of Ofgem formula. Most modern UK meters.

📐 Imperial (ft³) Formula

kWh = ft³ × 0.02832 × CV × 1.02264 ÷ 3.6

First converts ft³ to m³ (1 ft³ = 0.028317 m³), then applies Ofgem formula.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a gas unit?
A gas unit is simply the measurement your gas meter counts in. For metric meters, 1 unit = 1 cubic metre (m³) of gas. For imperial meters, 1 unit = 1 cubic foot (ft³) of gas. Your energy bill converts these units to kilowatt hours (kWh) for billing purposes using the Ofgem formula.
Do I have an imperial or metric gas meter?
Check the unit label on your meter display. Metric meters show m³ or the letter M after the reading. Imperial meters show ft³ or CF. If you're unsure, check your gas bill — it will state the meter type and the conversion factor used. Most meters installed after 1994 are metric.
What does 1 unit of gas cost in the UK?
Gas is billed in pence per kWh. Under the Ofgem price cap (as of 2025), the typical unit rate is approximately 6–8p per kWh. Since 1 m³ ≈ 11.18 kWh, each cubic metre of gas costs approximately 67–89p at current rates. Use our Gas Bill Calculator for exact cost estimates.
How many kWh does a typical UK home use per year?
According to Ofgem, average annual gas usage in the UK is approximately: 1-bed flat: 8,000 kWh; 2-bed house: 11,500 kWh; 3-bed house: 14,000 kWh; 4+ bed house: 18,000+ kWh. Usage varies significantly by property size, insulation, and household behaviour.
