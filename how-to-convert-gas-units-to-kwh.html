How to Convert Gas Units to kWh: Complete UK Guide

A comprehensive step-by-step guide to converting gas meter readings into kilowatt hours (kWh), using the official Ofgem formula that all UK energy suppliers are required to use.

Understanding how your gas meter reading translates into kilowatt hours is essential for checking your energy bills, budgeting accurately, and understanding your energy consumption. The UK energy regulator Ofgem mandates a specific conversion method that accounts for the energy content of gas (calorific value) and atmospheric correction factors. This guide explains every step in plain English, with worked examples for both metric and imperial meters.

What Are Gas Units?

Gas units are the raw measurements that your gas meter records. There are two types of gas meters in UK homes:

📏

Metric Meters (m³)

Display readings in cubic metres (m³). Installed in most UK homes since the 1990s. The meter display shows a small "m³" label. 1 unit = 1 cubic metre.

📐

Imperial Meters (ft³)

Display readings in cubic feet (ft³ or CF). Found in older UK properties. Numbers run higher (e.g., 40,000–60,000). 1 unit = 1 cubic foot.

Gas meters don't measure energy directly — they measure volume. The conversion to kWh accounts for the fact that the same volume of gas contains different amounts of energy depending on its composition and pressure.

The Official UK Gas Conversion Formula

Ofgem (the Office of Gas and Electricity Markets) specifies the exact formula that all licensed UK gas suppliers must use. This ensures bills are calculated consistently and fairly across all suppliers:

Ofgem Official Formula — Metric (m³)
kWh = m³ used × Calorific Value × 1.02264 ÷ 3.6
m³ used
The difference between your current and previous meter readings. This is the volume of gas consumed.
Calorific Value
How much energy is in 1 m³ of gas (kWh/m³). UK average: 39.5. Printed on your gas bill.
1.02264 (VCF)
Volume Correction Factor. Adjusts for the difference between gas at meter pressure and standard conditions.

The divisor 3.6 converts megajoules (MJ) to kilowatt hours (kWh). 1 kWh = 3.6 MJ, so dividing by 3.6 converts the result to kWh.

Step-by-Step: How to Convert Gas M3 to kWh

1
Read Your Gas Meter
Write down your current meter reading. Subtract your previous reading (from your last bill or meter photo). This gives you units used.
Example: Current = 1,250 m³, Previous = 1,180 m³, Units used = 70 m³
2
Find Your Calorific Value
Look at your gas bill — in the usage breakdown section, find the calorific value. It will be something like "CV: 39.8 kWh/m³". If you don't have a bill, use 39.5 as a UK average estimate.
Example: CV = 39.8 kWh/m³
3
Apply the Ofgem Formula
Multiply: units × CV × 1.02264, then divide by 3.6.
Calculation: 70 × 39.8 × 1.02264 ÷ 3.6
= 70 × 39.8 = 2,786
= 2,786 × 1.02264 = 2,849.07
= 2,849.07 ÷ 3.6 = 791.41 kWh
✓
Verify Your Result
Compare your calculated kWh with the kWh shown on your gas bill. If they match (within 1–2 kWh), your bill is correct. Small differences may occur due to rounding.

Step-by-Step: How to Convert Imperial Gas Units (ft³) to kWh

Imperial meters measure in cubic feet. An extra conversion step is needed before applying the Ofgem formula:

Imperial Formula — Cubic Feet to kWh
kWh = ft³ used × 0.028317 × Calorific Value × 1.02264 ÷ 3.6

Example: Imperial meter: Current = 50,000 ft³, Previous = 49,295 ft³ → Used = 705 ft³

  1. Units used: 705 ft³
  2. Convert to m³: 705 × 0.028317 = 19.963 m³
  3. Multiply by CV: 19.963 × 39.5 = 788.52
  4. Multiply by VCF: 788.52 × 1.02264 = 806.38
  5. Divide by 3.6: 806.38 ÷ 3.6 = 224.0 kWh

How to Find Your Calorific Value

Your calorific value (CV) is the single most important variable in the gas conversion formula. Here's where to find it:

  1. On your gas bill: Look in the "Gas used" or "Consumption details" section for a line like "Calorific value: 39.3 kWh/m³"
  2. On your supplier's website: Most suppliers publish their current calorific values
  3. National Grid: The National Grid publishes calorific values by Local Distribution Zone
  4. Default estimate: If you can't find it, use 39.5 kWh/m³ as the UK average (accurate to within ±3% for most properties)

Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Using wrong CV
Using 39.5 when your actual CV is 40.2 will make your calculation about 1.8% off. Always use your bill's exact CV for precise results.
❌ Forgetting the VCF
Some people skip the 1.02264 factor. This will give a result about 2.2% lower than your bill shows. The VCF is mandatory in the Ofgem formula.
❌ Imperial meters without ft³ → m³ conversion
If you have an imperial meter and don't multiply by 0.028317 first, your result will be wildly off (about 35× too large).
❌ Using kWh/m³ as the divisor
The formula divides by 3.6 (fixed constant). Never replace 3.6 with your CV or any other variable — 3.6 converts MJ to kWh and is always 3.6.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is 1 unit of gas in kWh?
For a metric meter, 1 unit = 1 m³ ≈ 11.18 kWh (at UK average CV of 39.5). For an imperial meter, 1 unit = 1 ft³ ≈ 0.317 kWh. The exact value depends on your calorific value.
Can I use a simplified version of the formula?
For quick estimates, you can use: kWh ≈ m³ × 11.18 (using UK average CV with VCF built in). This is accurate to within about 2% for most UK households. For bill verification, always use the full formula with your exact CV.
Why does Ofgem use the volume correction factor?
Gas expands and contracts with temperature and pressure. The gas at your meter is at atmospheric pressure (about 1 mbar above atmospheric), while gas is measured at standard conditions (1.01325 bar, 15°C). The VCF of 1.02264 corrects for this difference, ensuring you're billed for the actual energy content rather than just the volume at meter pressure.
How do I know if I have a metric or imperial gas meter?
Check the unit label on your meter display. Metric meters show m³ or the letter M. Imperial meters show ft³ or CF. Most meters installed after 1994 are metric. You can also check your gas bill — it will state the meter type and include a conversion factor (imperial bills typically show a factor of around 2.83).
Do all UK gas suppliers use the same formula?
Yes. Ofgem mandates the same formula (kWh = m³ × CV × 1.02264 ÷ 3.6) for all licensed UK gas suppliers. The only variable that differs between suppliers is the calorific value, which reflects the actual energy content of gas in their network area.
What should I do if my calculated kWh doesn't match my bill?
First, ensure you're using the exact CV from your bill (not the 39.5 estimate). Check you've entered the correct meter readings. If the discrepancy is more than 5 kWh on a typical monthly bill, contact your energy supplier and ask them to explain the calculation. You have a right under Ofgem's supply licence conditions to a full breakdown of your bill.
Is the formula the same for business gas accounts?
The conversion formula is the same (it's a physical calculation, not a tariff choice). However, business energy tariffs are not covered by the Ofgem domestic price cap — businesses negotiate rates directly. The VCF may also differ for industrial customers on high-pressure supply systems.
Can I convert gas units to kWh without knowing the calorific value?
Yes — use the UK average of 39.5 kWh/m³. This gives a result within about 2–3% of the true value for most properties. The simplified formula becomes: kWh ≈ m³ × 11.18. Use our Gas M3 to kWh Calculator which defaults to 39.5 but lets you enter your exact value.
