M3 to kWh Conversion: Complete UK Guide

Understanding how to convert natural gas from cubic metres to kilowatt hours is essential for UK householders, landlords, and energy managers. This guide covers the official Ofgem formula, a large reference table, and a free calculator.

When your gas meter shows a reading in cubic metres (m³), your energy supplier converts this volume into kilowatt hours (kWh) for billing. This conversion is not simply a fixed multiplication — it requires knowing the energy content (calorific value) of the gas in your area and applying a standardised correction factor. Understanding this process helps you cross-check your bills, budget accurately, and compare energy suppliers on a fair basis.

The Official Ofgem Conversion Formula

Ofgem, the UK energy regulator, mandates a specific formula for converting gas volume to energy. All licensed UK gas suppliers must use this formula when calculating the kWh figures on your bill:

Ofgem Mandated Formula
kWh = m³ × Calorific Value (kWh/m³) × 1.02264 ÷ 3.6

The three components are: Calorific Value (CV) — the energy content of 1 m³ of gas (varies by region and season, typically 39.5 kWh/m³); 1.02264 — the Volume Correction Factor (VCF) that adjusts for the fact that gas at your meter pressure differs from gas at standard conditions; 3.6 — a fixed constant converting megajoules to kilowatt hours (1 kWh = 3.6 MJ).

Step-by-Step Conversion Example

Scenario: Your current meter reads 1,250 m³ and previous reading was 1,180 m³. Your bill shows CV = 39.8 kWh/m³.

  1. Calculate units used: 1,250 − 1,180 = 70 m³
  2. Multiply by CV: 70 × 39.8 = 2,786 kWh (gross)
  3. Multiply by VCF: 2,786 × 1.02264 = 2,849.07
  4. Divide by 3.6: 2,849.07 ÷ 3.6 = 791.41 kWh
Result: 791.41 kWh used

Factors That Affect the Conversion

📍 Regional Variation
The calorific value varies by region because different pipeline networks carry gas with slightly different compositions. Northern networks may carry gas with a CV of 38.5, while southern networks may be as high as 41.0.
🌡️ Seasonal Variation
Gas CV can vary by up to 2 kWh/m³ between summer and winter. LNG supplies (common in winter peak demand) have a slightly different CV to North Sea gas. Your supplier measures and reports the actual CV.
🔧 Pressure & Temperature
Gas at your meter is at different pressure than at standard conditions. The Volume Correction Factor (1.02264) adjusts for this. It's set by Ofgem and is the same for all domestic meters.
📄 Billed vs Actual
Some suppliers use a 30-day average CV for billing rather than the exact daily CV. This is permitted by Ofgem and typically results in differences of less than 0.5% from the true value.

British Gas M3 to kWh

British Gas, like all Ofgem-regulated suppliers, is legally required to use the same standard formula for converting m³ to kWh. The exact conversion on a British Gas bill will use your specific regional calorific value rather than the 39.5 average, which is why your bill might show a slightly different result than this calculator if you're using the default CV. Always enter the CV printed on your British Gas bill for the most accurate comparison.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is the 39.5 kWh/m³ default?
The 39.5 kWh/m³ figure is the UK average and is accurate to within 2–4% for most properties. If you want exact results that match your bill, use the CV shown on your gas bill — typically shown in the usage breakdown as "calorific value: XX.X kWh/m³".
Does the formula change for different types of gas?
The formula structure is the same for all natural gas, but the calorific value varies. Natural gas (methane-rich North Sea or LNG) has a CV of roughly 37–43 kWh/m³. Biogas/biomethane blended into the grid has similar CV. Liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) has a much higher CV and uses a different formula.
Why does my bill show a different result to this calculator?
The most common reason is that the calculator uses the default CV of 39.5, while your bill uses your actual regional CV. Enter your bill's exact CV into the calculator and the results should match very closely (within rounding differences).
Can I use this for LPG (propane/butane) conversions?
No. LPG has a very different calorific value (propane ≈ 93.1 kWh/m³, butane ≈ 122.8 kWh/m³) and the Ofgem VCF does not apply. This calculator is for natural gas (mains gas) only. LPG customers should use the CV provided by their LPG supplier.
What is the kWh equivalent of 1 m3 of gas?
At the UK standard CV of 39.5 kWh/m³: (1 × 39.5 × 1.02264) ÷ 3.6 = 11.18 kWh. This is the figure most commonly cited for UK domestic natural gas.
How do I check my gas bill is calculated correctly?
1. Find the m³ reading difference on your bill. 2. Note the calorific value stated on your bill. 3. Apply the formula: kWh = m³ × CV × 1.02264 ÷ 3.6. 4. Compare your result to the kWh shown on the bill — it should match within 1–2 kWh. If it differs significantly, contact your supplier.
