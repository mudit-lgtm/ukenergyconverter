❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert gas m3 to kWh?
Multiply the m³ reading by the calorific value (typically 39.5 kWh/m³), then multiply by 1.02264 (volume correction factor), and divide by 3.6 to get kWh. The standard UK formula is: kWh = m³ × calorific value × 1.02264 ÷ 3.6. For example, 20 m³ at CV 39.5 = (20 × 39.5 × 1.02264) ÷ 3.6 = 224.15 kWh.
What is the calorific value of gas in the UK?
The calorific value varies by region and season but is typically between 37.5 and 43.0 kWh/m³. The standard Ofgem value used for billing is approximately 39.5 kWh/m³. Your exact calorific value is printed on your gas bill — look for "CV" or "calorific value" in the usage breakdown section.
How many kWh is 1 m3 of gas?
1 m³ of natural gas is approximately 11.18 kWh using the standard UK calorific value of 39.5 kWh/m³ and the Ofgem volume correction factor of 1.02264. The exact calculation: (1 × 39.5 × 1.02264) ÷ 3.6 = 11.18 kWh.
What does 1 unit of gas equal in kWh?
For imperial (cubic feet) meters, 1 unit = 1 cubic foot. For metric (m³) meters, 1 unit = 1 cubic metre. In kWh, 1 m³ ≈ 11.18 kWh and 1 cubic foot ≈ 0.317 kWh using the UK standard formula. Metric meters are now standard, but many older UK properties still have imperial meters.
How do I read my gas meter?
Read the dial or digital display from left to right. Ignore the red digits or digits after a decimal point on older dial meters. Subtract your previous reading from the current reading to get units used. For digital meters, simply note the number shown (excluding digits after the decimal point). Smart meters send readings automatically.
Is this calculator accurate for British Gas bills?
Yes. The formula uses the same Ofgem-mandated calculation method that British Gas, Octopus Energy, EDF, Scottish Power, and all UK regulated suppliers use. Results may vary slightly depending on your specific calorific value, which changes seasonally. Always use the CV printed on your bill for the most accurate result.
