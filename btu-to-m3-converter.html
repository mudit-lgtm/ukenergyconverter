BTU and M3 Explained

A British Thermal Unit (BTU) is the amount of heat needed to raise the temperature of one pound of water by one degree Fahrenheit. Despite the name, BTU is now most commonly used in the United States for heating/cooling systems and gas appliances. In the UK, BTU is still used for appliance ratings (e.g., boiler output) though the SI unit joule (J) or kilowatt (kW) is standard.

One cubic metre of natural gas contains approximately 35,900 BTU (varying slightly by calorific value). This is equivalent to 10.55 kWh or 37.55 MJ.

BTU to M3 Formula (Natural Gas)
m³ = BTU ÷ 35,900
M3 to BTU Formula
BTU = m³ × 35,900
